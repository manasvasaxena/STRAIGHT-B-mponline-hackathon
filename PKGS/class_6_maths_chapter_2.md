### 1. Fundamental Geometric Concepts

#### 1.1 The Point
* **Definition:** A point determines a precise location in space or on a plane.
* **Characteristics:** It has no length, width, or thickness (dimensionless).
* **Representation:** Represented by a fine dot drawn with a sharp pencil and named using a single capital letter, such as $P$, $Q$, or $A$.

#### 1.2 Line Segment
* **Definition:** The shortest path connecting two distinct points.
* **Notation:** A line segment between points $A$ and $B$ is denoted as $\overline{AB}$ or $\overline{BA}$.
* **Key Properties:**
  * Has two fixed endpoints ($A$ and $B$).
  * Has a definite, measurable length.

```
       A •-------------------------• B
                 Segment AB
```

#### 1.3 Line
* **Definition:** A line segment extended indefinitely in both directions without an end.
* **Notation:** A line passing through points $A$ and $B$ is denoted as $\overleftrightarrow{AB}$, or by a single lowercase letter like $l$, $m$, or $n$.
* **Key Properties:**
  * Has no endpoints.
  * Cannot be measured because its length is infinite.
  * Through any single given point, infinitely many lines can pass.
  * Through any two distinct points, exactly **one unique line** can pass.

```
     <-------•-----------------•------->
             A                 B
                          Line AB
```

#### 1.4 Ray
* **Definition:** A portion of a line that begins at a fixed starting point (origin) and extends endlessly in one direction.
* **Notation:** A ray starting at $O$ and extending through point $A$ is written as $\vec{OA}$.
  > **Note:** The starting point is always written first ($\vec{OA} \neq \vec{AO}$).
* **Key Properties:**
  * Has exactly one endpoint (the origin/vertex).
  * Has no fixed length.

```
       O •------------------------->
       (origin)                    A
                  Ray OA
```

### 2. Summary Comparison: Line, Segment, and Ray

| Property | Point | Line Segment ($\overline{AB}$) | Line ($\overleftrightarrow{AB}$) | Ray ($\vec{AB}$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Number of Endpoints** | $0$ | $2$ | $0$ | $1$ |
| **Definite Length?** | No | Yes | No | No |
| **Can be drawn on paper?** | Yes | Yes | Can only be represented | Can only be represented |
| **Extensions** | None | None | Both directions infinitely | One direction infinitely |

### 3. Understanding Angles

#### 3.1 What is an Angle?
An **angle** is formed when two rays share a common starting point.
* **Arms / Sides:** The two rays forming the angle (e.g., ray $BA$ and ray $BC$).
* **Vertex:** The common initial point where the two rays meet (point $B$).
* **Notation:** The symbol for an angle is $\angle$. It is named using three letters with the vertex in the middle:
  $$\angle ABC \quad \text{or} \quad \angle CBA$$
  *(If there is no ambiguity, it may also be named simply as $\angle B$.)*

```
             A
            /
           /
          /
         B •-------------------- C
         Vertex: B | Arms: BA, BC
```

### 4. Classification of Angles

Angles are measured in units called **degrees** ($^\circ$). Based on their measure, angles are classified into:

```
                      Types of Angles
                             │
     ┌───────────────┬───────┴───────┬───────────────┐
     ▼               ▼               ▼               ▼
   Acute           Right          Obtuse         Straight
(0° < θ < 90°)    (θ = 90°)    (90° < θ < 180°)  (θ = 180°)
```

1. **Zero Angle:**
   * Measure: Exactly $0^\circ$ (the two arms coincide without rotation).
2. **Acute Angle:**
   * Measure: Greater than $0^\circ$ and less than $90^\circ$.
   * Example: $30^\circ, 45^\circ, 60^\circ, 85^\circ$.
3. **Right Angle:**
   * Measure: Exactly $90^\circ$.
   * Visual indicator: Looks like an "L" shape (arms are perpendicular).
4. **Obtuse Angle:**
   * Measure: Greater than $90^\circ$ and less than $180^\circ$.
   * Example: $100^\circ, 120^\circ, 150^\circ$.
5. **Straight Angle:**
   * Measure: Exactly $180^\circ$.
   * Visual indicator: The arms form a single straight line.
6. **Reflex Angle:**
   * Measure: Greater than $180^\circ$ and less than $360^\circ$.
   * Example: $210^\circ, 270^\circ, 315^\circ$.
7. **Complete Angle:**
   * Measure: Exactly $360^\circ$ (one full rotation).

### 5. Clocks and Angles (Application Concepts)

A standard clock face is a full circle divided into $12$ equal hour marks:
$$\text{Full turn} = 360^\circ$$
$$\text{Angle between two consecutive numbers} = \frac{360^\circ}{12} = 30^\circ$$

* **At 1 o'clock:** $1 \times 30^\circ = 30^\circ$ (Acute angle)
* **At 2 o'clock:** $2 \times 30^\circ = 60^\circ$ (Acute angle)
* **At 3 o'clock:** $3 \times 30^\circ = 90^\circ$ (Right angle)
* **At 4 o'clock:** $4 \times 30^\circ = 120^\circ$ (Obtuse angle)
* **At 6 o'clock:** $6 \times 30^\circ = 180^\circ$ (Straight angle)

### 6. Practice Exercises & Solutions

#### Q1. How many lines can pass through:
* **(a)** One given point?
  * **Answer:** Infinitely many lines.
* **(b)** Two given points?
  * **Answer:** Only one unique line.

#### Q2. Classify the following angle measures:
* $35^\circ \rightarrow$ **Acute angle**
* $90^\circ \rightarrow$ **Right angle**
* $145^\circ \rightarrow$ **Obtuse angle**
* $180^\circ \rightarrow$ **Straight angle**
* $230^\circ \rightarrow$ **Reflex angle**

#### Q3. What is the angle between the hour hand and the minute hand of a clock at 9:00?
* **Solution:**
  At 9:00, the minute hand is at 12 and the hour hand is at 9.
  $$\text{Interval} = 3 \text{ spaces}$$
  $$\text{Angle} = 3 \times 30^\circ = 90^\circ \quad (\text{Right angle})$$

# Chapter 2: Whole Numbers

---

### 1. Definitions
* **Natural Numbers:** Counting numbers starting from $1$:
  $$\mathbb{N} = \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5, \dots\}$$
* **Whole Numbers:** All natural numbers together with zero ($0$):
  $$\mathbb{W} = \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, \dots\}$$
  * Smallest natural number: $1$
  * Smallest whole number: $0$
  * Every natural number is a whole number, but $0$ is not a natural number.

### 2. Predecessor and Successor
* **Predecessor:** Number obtained by subtracting $1$ from a given number ($n - 1$).
  * The whole number $0$ has no predecessor in whole numbers.
* **Successor:** Number obtained by adding $1$ to a given number ($n + 1$).

### 3. Properties of Whole Numbers

1. **Closure Property:**
   * Whole numbers are closed under addition and multiplication.
   * If $a, b \in \mathbb{W}$, then $(a + b) \in \mathbb{W}$ and $(a \times b) \in \mathbb{W}$.
   * *Not closed* under subtraction and division.

2. **Commutative Property:**
   * Addition: $a + b = b + a$
   * Multiplication: $a \times b = b \times a$

3. **Associative Property:**
   * Addition: $(a + b) + c = a + (b + c)$
   * Multiplication: $(a \times b) \times c = a \times (b \times c)$

4. **Distributive Property of Multiplication over Addition:**
   $$a \times (b + c) = (a \times b) + (a \times c)$$

5. **Identities:**
   * **Additive Identity:** $0$ ($a + 0 = a$)
   * **Multiplicative Identity:** $1$ ($a \times 1 = a$)
   * **Division by Zero:** Division of any number by $0$ is **undefined** ($a \div 0$ is undefined).
